A 15-minute standup, split

Run /pace, set 15 minutes and divide the time into three modules. The Scrum Guide sets the Daily Scrum at 15 minutes, and this split gives each part of it a limit.

15%60%25%
15%2 min 15 s

Context

Remind the team of the sprint goal and anything that changed overnight.

60%9 min

Updates

Each person shares progress and plans. Nine minutes keeps updates short enough to stay useful.

25%3 min 45 s

Blockers

Raise what is stuck and who can help. Naming an owner matters more than solving it during the call.

Why standups run long

1

One topic grows

A problem gets debated in front of everyone when it could be taken offline.

2

Nobody owns the clock

The facilitator ends up cutting people off. A visible bar shares that job with the room.

3

Blockers arrive last and rushed

With 25% of the time reserved, blockers get a real slot instead of the final minute.

4

Updates sprawl

A short fixed time keeps each update on what changed since yesterday.

Set it up once

  1. 1

    Open the modal

Standup questions

How long should a daily standup be?

The Scrum Guide describes the Daily Scrum as a 15-minute event. HuddlePace helps you hold that limit.

Does the speaker get a private alert?

Yes. The active speaker receives a Slack DM at the midpoint and with one minute left.

Can I reuse the same standup every day?

Yes. Save it as a template and start from it in one click.

Does HuddlePace record audio?

No. It records 0% audio and stores no transcripts. It keeps only the timestamps and the agenda modules it needs to run the timer.
