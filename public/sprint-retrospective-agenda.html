The 20/50/30 split

Here is how 60 minutes divide. Change the total in the modal and the minutes follow the percentages.

20%12 min

Metrics review

Look at what the sprint produced: finished work, carry-over, cycle time and incidents. Facts come first, so the discussion starts from one shared picture.

50%30 min

Improvement discussion

Pick the two or three topics the team cares about most and work them properly. Thirty minutes covers a few topics well and ten topics badly.

30%18 min

Agreements and action items

Turn the discussion into commitments. Every item gets an owner and a date before this block ends.

Four ways a retro goes off the rails

1

The first topic eats the hour

A visible bar tells the room how much of the block is left, so nobody has to be the person who interrupts.

2

Metrics turn into a debate

Twelve minutes is enough to read the numbers. Arguments about causes belong to the discussion block.

3

Action items arrive in the last two minutes

Giving them 30% of the time puts them on the clock instead of the leftovers.

4

Everyone leaves with a different list

The Huddle thread stays open, so the team can write the action items where everyone sees them.

Retrospective questions

How long should a sprint retrospective be?

The Scrum Guide caps it at three hours for a one-month sprint and treats it as shorter for shorter sprints. Many two-week teams use 60 minutes. The percentages scale to any length.

Can I change the percentages?

Yes. Set any split in the schedule modal. The modules must add up to 100%, and when you edit one value HuddlePace rebalances the others.

What happens if a block finishes early?

The speaker taps Next Module and the leftover time moves to the next block.

Does HuddlePace record the retro?

No. It records 0% audio and keeps only the timestamps and the agenda modules it needs to run the timer.
